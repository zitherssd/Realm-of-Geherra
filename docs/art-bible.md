## Section 1: Visual Identity Statement

### Selected Direction: Antediluvian Earth

**One-line visual rule:** Grounded, earthy pixel art with a bronze-age-meets-high-magic aesthetic — think Mycenaean frescoes crossbred with Sumerian cylinder seals, where every gemstone glows and every god-touched creature is visibly *other*.

### Supporting Visual Principles

**1. Earth-Tech Visual Language**
Architecture and technology use cyclopean masonry, timber, clay, bronze, and gem inlays. No iron plate armour, no steel swords — think layered linen, bronze scale, obsidian-edged weapons. Gems are socketed into tools, weapons, and armour as power conduits.

*Design test:* "Does this object look like it belongs to a civilisation that mastered gem-magic instead of electricity?"

**2. God-Touched Creatures**
Monsters and giants are visually distinct from natural fauna — they have extra eyes, crystalline growths, iridescent patches, or anatomical impossibilities. A giant isn't just a big human; a sphinx has lapis-lazuli veins in its wings.

*Design test:* "At a glance, can you tell this creature was shaped by divine experimentation rather than natural evolution?"

**3. Faction Identity Through Material & Motif**
Each faction's visual identity is rooted in a distinct material and decorative language:

- **Pelasgians (proto-Dacian/Indo-European)**: Wool, leather, bronze torcs, spiral motifs, forest colours (deep greens, ochre, copper)
- **Proto-Mycenaeans**: Linen, boar-tusk helms, octopus/pillar motifs, Aegean blues and rust reds
- **Proto-Egyptians (Middle Eastern)**: Linen kilts, lapis lazuli, gold, scarab and lotus motifs, desert ochres and indigos

*Design test:* "From colour and silhouette alone, can you identify which faction a unit belongs to?"

**4. Gemlight as Magic Language**
Magic and skill effects are expressed through gemstone colours and crystalline geometry. A divination spell pulses with moonstone white. A fire invocation glows like a ruby. Engineering constructs hum with amber light.

*Design test:* "Is every magical effect visually tied to a gemstone or earth-material, not to generic sparkles?"

### Colour Philosophy

A warm, earthy base palette — clay reds, amber, ochre, verdigris, deep indigo. Faction colours are natural pigments (copper, lapis, cinnabar). Magic and gems introduce saturated, luminous accents (emerald green, sapphire blue, ruby red, amethyst purple) that pop against the grounded world. Monsters have unnatural colour shifts — a giant scorpion's carapace might catch light with an opalescent sheen.

## Section 3: Shape Language

### 1. Character Silhouette Philosophy

**Core principle:** *Readability at a glance, distinctiveness from three tiles away.*

In a tactical pixel-art game where the camera may zoom out to show a formation of 20+ units, **silhouette is the primary identifier** — colour is secondary. Every unit archetype and faction must be identifiable from its outline alone, even at 24×32 pixels.

**Faction Silhouette Rules:**

| Faction | Silhouette Character | Head Profile | Base/Stance | Example Motif |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pelasgians** | Broad, slumped, rounded — hides and wool create bulk with no sharp edges | Rounded dome helms, wild hair bursting outward | Wide stance, slightly hunched — grounded, forest-dwelling | A bear-like silhouette — wide shoulders, no waist definition |
| **Proto-Mycenaeans** | Tapered, athletic, angular — linen and bronze scale create a V-torso profile | Boar-tusk helms with tall crests, distinct cheek guards | Narrow stance, upright — maritime, disciplined | An inverted triangle — broad shoulders, narrow hips, crested helm extending height |
| **Proto-Egyptians** | Regal, geometric, erect — kilts and bare chest create clean horizontal/vertical lines | Tall hedjet-style crowns or striped nemes headdresses | Even stance, perfectly vertical spine — ritualistic, unmoving | A rectangle with a triangular headdress — man-made, architectural |

**Archetype Silhouette Rules (cross-faction):**

- **Captain / Hero**: Tallest silhouette in the unit. Head extends above the standard pixel cap. Distinctive headwear (crest, crown, horned helm) breaks the skyline. A cape or streamer adds horizontal width and movement.
- **Infantry**: Compact, square-ish, shield-forward. The shield creates a vertical slab on one side of the body. Spear adds an upward diagonal thrust beyond the frame. Read as a **wall segment**.
- **Archer / Skirmisher**: Asymmetrical — one arm raised to draw, bow creates a crescent gap in the silhouette. Leaner torso, narrower base. Read as a **mobile threat**.
- **Monster / God-Touched**: A single distinguishing trait that makes them different — never multiple mutations. A creature may have one extra feature (crystal growth, disproportionate limb, unusual head shape) but otherwise follows natural anatomy. These are recognisable ancestors of the mythical creatures we know today.
- **Giant**: Extends beyond the standard unit tile — occupies 2×1 or 2×2 tiles. Head is proportionally smaller than a human's. Massive weapon acts as a third structural element.

> **Pillar connection — *Preparation Over Reflexes***: Clean, readable silhouettes let the player assess enemy formations at a glance before committing. A player scanning the battlefield should be able to count archers vs. infantry and identify captains in under two seconds.

### 2. Environment Geometry

**Dominant geometry: Cyclopean-Organic Blend — massive angular blocks intercut by flowing god-sculpted curves.**

The world is built by two forces: **human hands** (cyclopean masonry) and **divine will** (god-sculpted terrain). The visual tension between these two is the defining environmental theme.

**Human-made (Earth-Tech):**
- **Cyclopean masonry** — Irregular polygonal stone blocks, 5–7 sides, fitted without mortar. Each block is unique. Joints are dark, deep crevices.
- **Architecture** — Trapezoidal doorways, corbelled arches, monolithic lintels. Columns are unfluted, tapering, slightly irregular.
- **Surface treatment** — Deep chisel marks. Worn edges. Moss and lichen in the crevices.
- **Motifs carved into stone** — Spirals (Pelasgian), octopus/pillar (Mycenaean), scarab/lotus (Egyptian). Incised into the stone, not raised.

**God-sculpted (Antediluvian terrain):**
- **Organic fluidity** — Rock formations that look like frozen waves or vertebrae ridges. Terrain that cannot be explained by natural erosion.
- **Crystalline seams** — Veins of gemstone running through cliff faces, glowing faintly. These follow organic, branching paths.
- **Massive stone arches** that seem to defy gravity — remnants of divine architecture.

**Terrain silhouette guidelines:**
- Mountains have **flat tops and stepped sides** (mesa-like, ziggurat-like — not alpine peaks)
- Trees are **gnarled, twisting, asymmetrical** — Mediterranean olive/carob shapes
- Ruins are **massive in scale but fragmented** — a single standing stone wall should be 3× human height, broken and incomplete

> **Pillar connection — *A Living, Persistent World***: Terrain geometry tells a story of deep time — crumbled cyclopean walls, half-sunken god-sculpted arches, worn paths. The world has existed for millennia.

### 3. UI Shape Grammar

**Core principle:** *The UI is carved into the world, not floating above it.*

The interface should feel like it belongs to the Antediluvian world — clay tablets, bronze plaques, gemstone facets, and carved stone.

**Panel shapes:**
- **Primary panels**: Asymmetrical with irregular edges — like a cracked clay tablet. Corners are rounded but uneven. A subtle shadow suggests depth — the panel is *resting on* the world.
- **Tooltips / hover cards**: Small rectangular clay tags with a single rivet-hole at the top.
- **Dialog boxes**: Framed by a simplified meander/key pattern or scarab-border. Bronze-coloured with darker oxidation in the corners.

**Button shapes:**
- **Primary action buttons**: Shield-shaped or scarab-shaped — rounded top tapering to a blunt point at the bottom.
- **Secondary buttons**: Circular medallions like stamped bronze coins.
- **Toggle/checkbox**: A gemstone facet — hexagon or octagon outline. The "checked" state fills the facet with gem-coloured glow.

**Frame and border shapes:**
- **Window borders**: Double-line frame — outer line is thick and rough (carved stone), inner line is thin and precise (bronze inlay).
- **Decorative corners**: Each corner has a larger motif — a bronze rivet, a small carved animal head, or a geometric knot.
- **Separators**: Cuneiform-style wedge marks or incised lines.

**Typography integration:**
- Text sits on a darker, rectangular "field" within the panel — like an inscription tablet with faint horizontal guide-lines.
- Important numbers (HP, gold) are displayed in gemstone facets — numbers carved into the gem face.

**Faction-specific UI flourishes:**
- Pelasgian UI: Spiral borders, forest-green clay backgrounds, copper-riveted corners
- Proto-Mycenaean UI: Wave/octopus motifs, Aegean-blue fields
- Proto-Egyptian UI: Scarab-corner decorations, lotus-column side frames, lapis lazuli accents

> **Pillar connection — *Discovery-Driven Exploration***: The UI shapes tell you *where* you are. When you enter a new faction's territory, the interface motifs shift — your inventory panel is now framed with scarabs instead of spirals.

### 4. Hero Shapes vs. Supporting Shapes

**Silhouette hierarchy** — the eye is drawn by: height, width, asymmetry, and negative space.

**Heroes (party captain, named NPCs, legendary enemies):**
- **Height advantage**: Heroes are 4–6 pixels taller than standard units achieved through distinctive headgear that breaks the skyline.
- **Width through accessories**: Capes, cloaks, or trailing sashes add horizontal spread — more silhouette surface area.
- **Asymmetry as distinction**: A single pauldron, shield on one arm, or a banner on one side. This asymmetry catches the eye.
- **Animation complexity**: Heroes have 1–2 additional animation frames (cloak billow, crest bob) that standard units lack.

**Supporting units (standard troops, generic enemies):**
- **Standardized height**: All units of the same archetype share the same silhouette height.
- **Symmetric by default**: Standard troops are bilaterally symmetric — both arms same, both legs same. This makes them visually recede as a group.
- **Group readability**: When in formation, supporting units form a *texture* — repeating similar shapes the eye can group and dismiss.

**Danger communication through shape:**
- **Aggressive shapes** (enemy heroes, monsters): Pointed top silhouettes (spikes, horns), wide threatening stances, forward-leaning posture.
- **Defensive shapes** (player heroes, friendly units): Rounded top silhouettes (helms, hoods), upright or slightly back-leaning posture, shield-forward profiles.
- **Mystery/unknown** (POI, interactables): Shapes that are *unfinished* — partial circles, broken columns, half-excavated statues.

**Hero equipment shapes:**
- Weapon silhouette should extend *beyond the unit frame* — a hero's spear tip or sword point breaks the edge of their tile
- Gem socket locations on hero equipment should be visible at silhouette level — small protrusions where gems sit
- Heroes of high rank have a faint aura — a coloured sheen that follows their outline

> **Pillar connection — *Meaningful Progression***: As your captain gains renown and equipment, their silhouette evolves. A new gem-socketed bronze helm adds crest height. A legendary cloak adds trailing width. You literally *look* more important.

### 5. Monster / Giant Shape Rules

**Core principle:** *Primordial ancestors of the myths we know — grounded in natural anatomy, touched by divine experimentation, never grotesque for its own sake.*

God-touched creatures are the original templates. The Sphinx of Oedipus is a *descendant* of these primordial monsters. They are recognisable — a lion with wings, a giant with one eye — but rougher, more primeval, infused with materials (gemstone, bronze, crystal) by divine hands.

**What makes a god-touched creature:**

A god-touched creature applies **one, at most two** of the following modifications — subtlety is key. These should look like *natural creatures that were altered*, not chaotic experiments:

1. **Material infusion** — A patch of crystalline growth, a bronze-like hide, gemstone eyes, or mineral deposits in the creature's fur/hide/scales. The material looks *grafted on*, not born with. This is the most common divine modification.
2. **Single anatomical variation** — One feature is different from the natural template: an extra eye, a disproportionately large claw, a tail that shouldn't be there, a head slightly too large for the body. Never more than one such variation per creature.
3. **Proportion shift** — The creature follows a recognisable body plan but proportions are subtly wrong — longer limbs, broader torso, or a more hunched posture than the natural version. These are the genetic templates that would later become distinct species.
4. **Subtle geometric mark** — A perfect circle, spiral, or chevron pattern on the creature's body — a divine signature. Natural evolution doesn't produce perfect geometry.

**What they are NOT:**
- Not chaotic abominations with random extra limbs
- Not Lovecraftian horrors beyond comprehension
- Not chimeras of three different animals stitched together
- Not grotesque for shock value

**Monster silhouette archetypes (primordial templates):**

| Type | Silhouette Shape | Divine Modification | Mythological Descendant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primordial Sphinx** | Large cat-like base — horizontal, low, powerful | Lapis-lazuli veined wings, gemstone eyes that glow | The Sphinx of Thebes (Oedipus) |
| **Giant Scorpion** | Scorpion body plan — pincers forward, tail raised | Bronze-infused carapace with verdigris patina | The scorpions of myth and desert folklore |
| **Troll / Cave Giant** | Humanoid, hunched, thick-set — wider than tall | Protruding crystalline growths from spine/shoulders | Trolls of Norse and European myth |
| **Cyclops** | Humanoid giant — massive vertical pillar | Single central eye socket, bronze-studded hide | The Cyclops of Homer's Odyssey |
| **Giant (generic)** | Enlarged humanoid — occupies 2×1 or 2×2 tiles | Gem implants in skin, geometric tattoo brands | The Gigantes of Greek myth |
| **Griffin-like** | Eagle front + lion hind — winged | Feathers tipped with bronze-coloured barbs | The Griffin of classical mythology |

**Giant-specific rules:**
- **Scale communication**: A giant must *break the frame*. At minimum occupies 2×1 tiles (tall) or 2×2 tiles. Head is above the standard camera view.
- **Weapon proportion**: A giant's weapon should be the size of a human soldier. A club is a whole tree trunk.
- **Ground impact**: Giants have a wider base shadow than any other unit — a thick, dark ellipse.
- **Slow silhouette**: Giants use fewer animation frames, spaced farther apart. The slowness communicates mass.

> **Pillar connection — *Preparation Over Reflexes***: A monster's divine modifications are visible before combat begins. The player sees a creature with crystalline growths and can prepare accordingly — choose the right gem-enhanced weapon, the right formation. The visual itself is the warning.

### 6. Shape Decision Matrix

| Element | Dominant Shape | Why | Visual Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pelasgian infantry | Rounded rectangle | Hide/clothing creates bulk, no armour edges | Mycenaean warriors — bulkier, less defined |
| Mycenaean infantry | Inverted triangle | Boar-tusk helm + bronze scale creates V-shape | The Warrior Vase of Mycenae |
| Egyptian infantry | Upright rectangle | Kilts, bare chest, headdress creates clean lines | Narmer Palette figures |
| Monsters | Recognisable base + one modification | Primordial ancestors of known myths | Archaic Greek vase paintings |
| Cyclopean walls | Irregular polygons | Fitted random stone | Mycenaean Lion Gate masonry |
| God-sculpted rock | Fluid, organic curves | Contrast with human angularity | Hokusai's Great Wave in stone |
| UI panels | Irregular clay tablet shapes | Fired clay with chipped edges | Cuneiform tablet shapes |
| UI buttons | Shield / scarab / gem facet | Ancient iconography | Egyptian scarab amulets |
| Hero silhouette | Tall + wide + asymmetric | Breaks uniformity = importance | Homeric heroes described as "towering" |

### 4.1 Primary Palette (7 Core Colours)

These 7 colours form the foundational palette. Every visual element draws from them. Chosen for maximum distinction under deuteranopia — relying primarily on luminance separation and blue–yellow contrast.

### 4.2 Semantic Color Usage

| Semantic Role | Colour(s) | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Safe / Friendly / Healing | Verdigris + Parchment | Teal-green reads as calming; parchment is warm-neutral |
| Danger / Enemy / Hostile | Deep Indigo + darkened Terracotta | Ominous dark blue + dark blood-earth |
| Fire / Magic / Energy | Amber (warm), Verdigris (arcane) | Amber = physical fire. Verdigris = ancient magic |
| Gold / Wealth / Treasure | Amber | Single unambiguous gold |
| Death / Void | Charcoal + Deep Indigo | Absolute void + supernatural depth |
| Life / Growth | Forest Shade | Dark blue-green teal |
| Alert / Critical UI | Amber + shape icon | Redundant encoding |
| Interactive / Hover | Amber (light glow) | Glow effect + luminance increase |
| Disabled | Ochre at 40% opacity | Desaturation + reduced alpha |

### 4.3 Per-Biome Color Rules

**Plains** — Warm daylight. Sky: Parchment→pale amber. Ground: Terracotta at 70% saturation. Grass: Verdigris shifted warmer.

**Forest** — Canopy-filtered light. Sky: Parchment→Verdigris tint. Ground: darkened Terracotta with green shift. Foliage: Verdigris at full saturation.

**Wasteland** — Dust storms, ashen. Sky: Ochre→Charcoal gradient. Ground: desaturated Terracotta. Rock: Charcoal + Ochre highlight.

**Hills** — Clear, dusty. Sky: Parchment→pale Ochre. Sunlit: Ochre at 120%. Shadow: darkened Terracotta. Distant hills: Deep Indigo at 30% opacity.

**Desert** — Blazing. Sky: Amber→Parchment→white-hot. Sand: Ochre at 130%. Shadow: cooled Terracotta. Night: Deep Indigo + Amber star dots.

### 4.4 UI Palette

**UI Core:** Panel background = Parchment. Borders = Charcoal (outer) + Terracotta (inner). Text = Charcoal primary, Terracotta secondary, Amber highlighted.

**Button states:** Default = darkened Terracotta fill. Hover = Terracotta fill + Amber glow. Pressed = darker inset. Disabled = desaturated Ochre at 40%.

**Gemstone decorative accents** (6 gems, see §4.7): Used sparingly for borders, dividers, and title headers.

### 4.5 Colorblind Safety

All critical colour pairs verified for deuteranopia. Every pair has minimum Δ25 luminance difference plus blue–yellow hue backup. All text meets WCAG AA 4.5:1 contrast ratio.

**Testing protocol:** Apply a deuteranopia simulation filter to every screenshot. Verify all colour-encoded information is also available through luminance contrast (≥3:1), shape/icon redundancy, and position consistency.

## Section 7: UI/HUD Visual Direction

### 7.1 Diegetic Philosophy — Moderate-Diegetic Hybrid

Key information lives on-screen as styled panels, but the styling grounds everything in the Antediluvian world:
- **HUD elements** (health, party status, minimap) are screen-space overlays styled as clay tablets, gemstone facets, and bronze fixtures
- **Inventory, dialogue, quest log** are panel-based — opened like unrolling a clay tablet or lifting a bronze plaque
- **World map** exists as a physical scroll/tablet that occupies the full screen
- No floating damage numbers or health bars *on* characters in the world — damage feedback uses screen-space indicators styled as carved tally marks

### 7.2 Typography — Custom Pixel Font

A custom bitmap font at two weights:
- **Body text**: 6×8 pixel grid, used for dialogue, item descriptions, tooltips
- **Headers**: 8×10 pixel grid, used for screen titles, character names, important numbers
- Font style: Incised/inscribed look — think cuneiform wedge marks or Greek lapidary capitals. Letters have slightly uneven serifs suggesting chisel-cut stone
- All text sits on a slightly darker rectangular "field" within panels (like an inscription tablet)
- Numbers in gemstone facets for important values (HP, gold, mana)

### 7.3 Iconography — Tiered System

- **HUD-scale (16×16px)**: Outlined silhouettes with 1px dark stroke. Coloured fill indicates status (green = friendly, red = enemy, etc.)
- **Inventory-scale (24×24px)**: Gem-facet illustrations for magic items and faction badges. Outlined silhouettes for mundane items
- **Faction glyphs** (map and UI): 12×12px geometric devices — oak leaf (Pelasgians), wave spiral (Mycenaeans), sun disc (Egyptians)

### 7.4 UI Animation — Hybrid Approach

- **Panels and menus** (weighty stone): 250–400ms transitions with easing that simulates heavy stone moving. Panels slide in with a settling "thud" — 2–3px overshoot bounce at end. Subtle dust particles at panel edges on open/close
- **HUD elements** (snappy bronze): 120ms transitions. Health changes, damage numbers, resource ticks are immediate and responsive
- **Hover effects**: Tooltips fade in over 150ms. Interactive elements brighten with an amber glow (2px spread)
- **Button press**: Brief 50ms scale-down to 90% — feels like pressing a physical object

### 7.5 Key Screen Layouts

**HUD (in-battle):**
- Top-left: Party portraits (32×32px each, 1–6 in a column) with HP as notched tally-stick bars
- Top-right: Minimap (128×128px) in a circular bronze frame — shows terrain, party dots, POI icons
- Bottom-centre: Selected unit info panel — clay tablet with name, HP bar, action buttons
- Bottom-right: Quick action bar — 6 shield-shaped buttons (24×24px each)

**Inventory:**
- Full-screen clay tablet with irregular edges. Left: party member list. Centre: grid of items (faction-styled frame). Right: selected item detail with gemstone facet border for magic items
- Equipment slots arrayed around a central character silhouette (32×48px)

**Dialogue:**
- Bottom third of screen: Papyrus/tablet panel with speaker portrait (cameo-seal style with gemstone-inlaid border)
- Text scrolls in as typed inscription. Response options are shield-shaped buttons
- Speaker's faction gem colour subtly pulses in their portrait border

**World Map:**
- Full-screen scroll/tablet overlay. Terrain rendered in the game's colour palette with faction-coloured territory overlays
- Party position marker with heraldic sigil. POIs shown as faction glyphs

### 7.6 Faction UI Shifts

When interacting with a faction's town or characters, UI motifs shift:
- **Pelasgian UI**: Spiral borders, forest-green clay backgrounds, copper-riveted corners. Emerald gem accents
- **Mycenaean UI**: Wave/octopus motifs, Aegean-blue fields, figure-eight shield-shaped panels. Sapphire accents
- **Egyptian UI**: Scarab-corner decorations, lotus-column side frames, lapis lazuli accents. Topaz/gold highlights

The core layout remains identical — only decorative motifs and accent colours change — ensuring consistency while making faction identity omnipresent.

### 7.7 Retro Handheld Optimisations

- Minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for all text (verified by luminance)
- No information conveyed by colour alone — every state has shape/icon backup
- Critical icons (HP, mana, alert) use both colour AND distinct silhouette
- Button targets minimum 24×24px for touch/stylus usability on small screens
- No thin coloured lines (<2px) for information encoding — use fills and shapes
- All UI elements visible against Parchment background (maximally reflective for non-backlit screens)

## Section 8: Asset Standards

### 8.1 File Format & Pipeline

- **Sprites**: PNG format with indexed or full RGB colour. LÖVE loads PNGs natively via `love.graphics.newImage()`
- **Atlases**: Packed into sprite sheets. Atlas metadata stored as companion Lua modules exporting quad definitions
- **No mipmaps** — nearest-neighbour filtering, no 3D perspective
- **Pre-processing**: All assets go through a consistent PNG export pipeline with nearest-neighbour downscaling. No lossy compression (no JPEG for sprites)
- **AI-generated assets**: Must be hand-cleaned and palette-reduced to fit the game palette before import

### 8.2 Naming Convention

Format: `[type]_[faction]_[name]_[variant].[png|lua]`

Examples:
- `unit_pelasgian_infantry_01.png`
- `item_weapon_bronze_sword.png`
- `ui_panel_inventory_bg.png`
- `fx_gem_fire_ruby_glow.png`
- `portrait_mycenaean_captain.png`
- `tile_forest_ground_01.png`

Atlases use the same prefix with `_atlas` suffix: `units_pelasgian_atlas.png` with `units_pelasgian_atlas.lua`

### 8.3 Texture Resolution Tiers

| Asset Type | Base Resolution | Max Atlas Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| World map tiles | 16×16px | 2048×2048 | 3–5 colours per tile |
| Battle sprites (humanoid) | 32×32px per frame | 2048×2048 | 24–32px standing height |
| Battle sprites (giant) | 64×48px per frame | 2048×2048 | Occupies 2×2 tiles |
| UI icons (HUD) | 16×16px | 1024×1024 | Outlined silhouettes |
| UI icons (inventory) | 24×24px | 1024×1024 | Gem-facet for magic items |
| Portraits | 64×64px | 1024×1024 | Cameo-seal style |
| UI panels | 1024×768 or tileable | 2048×2048 | Clay tablet / papyrus backgrounds |
| Environment tiles | 32×32px | 2048×2048 | Seamless edges |
| Particle effects | 8×8px to 32×32px | 512×512 | Gem sparkles, dust motes, impact effects |

### 8.4 Sprite Sheet Organisation

- **Per-faction sheets**: All units of a faction packed together. Example: `units_pelasgian_atlas.png` contains all Pelasgian soldier types, idle/walk/attack frames
- **Per-category sheets**: UI elements on separate sheets from world sprites
- **Environment sheets**: Grouped by biome (plains tiles, forest tiles, etc.)
- **Shared sheets**: Neutral assets (particles, projectiles, common UI) packed together
- **Animation frames**: Laid out horizontally per sprite in the atlas. Frame width is consistent per sprite type

### 8.5 Animation Frame Standards

| Animation | Frames | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Idle | 2–4 | Subtle breathing/bobbing. 2 frames for standard units, 4 for captains/heroes (cape billow) |
| Walk | 4–6 | Full stride cycle. Giants use 4 frames at half speed |
| Attack (melee) | 4 | Wind-up (1), Strike (1), Recovery (2) |
| Attack (ranged) | 3 | Draw (1), Release (1), Follow-through (1) |
| Hit reaction | 2 | Knockback + recovery |
| Death | 3–4 | Stagger, fall, settle |
| Spell cast | 4 | Gesture (2), Release (1), Recovery (1) — with gem glow overlay |
| Giant attack | 4 | Slower — each frame held 2× longer than human attack frames |

**Performance cap:** Maximum 40 animated sprites on screen simultaneously at 60fps. Beyond that, distant/off-screen units use 2-frame simplified idle only.

### 8.6 Retro Handheld Optimisations

- **Max texture atlas dimension**: 2048×2048 (safe for GLES 2.0)
- **Palette per atlas**: ≤64 colours per atlas sheet
- **Memory budget per scene**: 128MB for textures (leaving the rest for code, audio, runtime)
- **SpriteBatch everything**: All repeated sprites rendered via `love.graphics.newSpriteBatch()` — this is the single most important optimisation for LÖVE
- **No runtime Canvas effects** on retro handhelds — pre-bake lighting and effects into sprite frames
- **Shaders**: Use GLSL ES compatible shaders only. Limit to 2 active shader uniforms per pass

### 8.7 Asset Budget Summary

| Category | Total Texture Memory | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| All unit sprites | ~32MB | 3 factions × 8 unit types × 4 animations × 4 frames × 32×32px |
| Environment tiles | ~16MB | 6 biomes × 8 tile types × 32×32px, seamless |
| UI elements | ~8MB | Panels, icons, portraits, fonts |
| Particles / FX | ~4MB | Gem glows, impact effects, weather |
| World map tiles | ~8MB | 16×16px tiles for procedural generation |
| **Total budget** | **~68MB** | Well within 256MB handheld limit with headroom |

### 8.8 Rendering Pipeline (LÖVE-Specific)

1. Load all atlases at scene start via `love.graphics.newImage()`
2. Create `SpriteBatch` objects per atlas per layer (ground, units, UI)
3. Each frame: clear batches, add visible sprites with transforms, draw batches
4. UI rendered last on separate Canvas layer
5. Nearest-neighbour filtering set globally via `love.graphics.setDefaultFilter('nearest', 'nearest')`

## Section 9: Reference Direction

### Reference 1: The Banner Saga (2014) — Weathered Textile & Worn Metal Rendering

**Draw from it**: The pixel art technique for rendering threadbare, aged textiles — look at portrait close-ups: warp and weft of wool cloaks suggested by 1-pixel horizontal dashes on a 2-pixel vertical ground, creating a "twill weave" illusion. Also the copper/brass patina technique: alternating 1-pixel rows of dark bronze and verdigris with random single-pixel specular hits to suggest hand-hammered (not cast) metal. This maps directly to Pelasgian wool garments and bronze torcs.

**Explicitly avoid**: The Norse/Viking iconography — runestones, dragon-prowed longships, carved knotwork. Our motif language is entirely different (spirals, rosettes, scarabs, wave motifs). Also avoid the stark black outline on characters — we want softer integration with backgrounds.

### Reference 2: Ōkami (2006) — Divine Brush-Stroke Magic Trails

**Draw from it**: The "Celestial Brush" technique — a divine stroke leaves a tapering ribbon of saturated colour that fades at its tail into glowing particles. Translate to pixel art: when a ruby-fire invocation is cast, the projectile trail should look like a calligraphic brush stroke of liquid fire — a 2-pixel-wide core at full saturation, thinning to 1 pixel, with single-pixel ember particles peeling off at the taper. When a gemstone magic effect persists (e.g. an amber shield), its edges should feather like wet ink bleeding into papyrus.

**Explicitly avoid**: The Japanese ink-wash watercolour aesthetic. The technique (tapered trails, bleeding edges, particle shedding) is what we borrow, not the art style. Also avoid super-deformed character proportions — our pixel scale is grounded and heroic.

### Reference 3: The Dig (1995, LucasArts) — Cyclopean Masonry Pixel Technique

**Draw from it**: How individual megalithic blocks are given visual weight. Each block has 3–4 subtly different brown/grey tones arranged in a "top-lit" gradient (lightest on top edge, darkest at bottom) with a 1-pixel highlight on the upper-left edge. No two adjacent blocks share the exact same gradient pattern, preventing a "tiled" look. This is the technique needed for Mycenaean cyclopean walls and Egyptian battered mudbrick.

**Explicitly avoid**: The sci-fi context (glowing alien consoles, crystal power conduits, transparent walkways). Our gems are socketed into stone, not floating in sci-fi panels. Also avoid the low-contrast atmosphere — our world is sunny, Mediterranean-bright with deep shadows.

### Reference 4: Akrotiri (Santorini) Frescoes, c. 1600 BCE — Minoan Colour Conventions & Decorative Motifs

**Draw from it**: The historical four-key pigment palette: (a) terra cotta (red iron oxide) for male figures and background bands, (b) Egyptian blue (calcium copper silicate — first synthetic pigment) for female figures, water, and sky, (c) saffron yellow (ochre) for decorative details and divine elements, and (d) carbon black for outlines. This maps directly to the Proto-Mycenaean faction. Also the compositional rule: figures in profile with full-frontal eyes, scenes framed by decorative borders of spirals, rosettes, or wave motifs.

**Explicitly avoid**: The "palatial cleanliness" — Akrotiri depicts a peaceful society at its zenith. Our world is waning, the flood approaches. Weather the frescoes: add crack lines, pigment flaking at edges, faded patches where colours have bleached.

### Reference 5: Sword & Sworcery (2009) — Atmospheric Screen Language & Pacing

**Draw from it**: The use of constrained pixel density for emotional weight — characters are small in the frame, backgrounds use negative space, screen transitions are slow and deliberate. Apply to our 1024×768 canvas: when transitioning between regions or entering a sacred site, use a 0.8-second "tablet turn" wipe (the screen rotates like a clay tablet turning over) rather than a hard cut. The pause between transitions should feel like the weight of a stone door grinding shut.

**Explicitly avoid**: The blue-purple nocturnal mood and 8-bit chiptune aesthetic. Our palette is warm-earth, not twilight-neon. Also avoid the self-aware/ironic tone — our world's atmosphere is mythic-epic and solemn.

### Reference Summary

| Reference | What It Teaches | Doesn't Overlap With |
|---|---|---|
| Banner Saga | Textile & metal pixel rendering | — |
| Ōkami | Magic trail technique & particle language | — |
| The Dig | Megalithic stone pixel technique | — |
| Akrotiri frescoes | Historical colour system & decorative rules | — |
| Sword & Sworcery | Screen language, pacing, transition weight | — |

## Section 10: Dialogue Writing Direction

### 10.1 Voice Target

Dialogue should combine:
- **Sophoclean elevation**: dignified, fateful, morally charged speech in moments of crisis
- **Xenophontic clarity**: direct, practical language for tactics, travel, trade, and command

The result is **formal but readable**: ancient in spirit, never opaque.

### 10.2 Core Tone Rules

1. **Speak in stakes, not chatter.** Even small talk should reveal duty, danger, reputation, hunger, weather, omens, or debt.
2. **Use concrete nouns.** Prefer "spear," "grain," "bronze," "oath," "river" over abstract filler.
3. **Keep lines compact.** Most lines should be 6-18 words. Reserve long lines for vows, prophecies, and turning points.
4. **Favor moral framing.** Characters weigh honor vs survival, oath vs expedience, kin vs glory.
5. **Avoid modern idiom.** No slang, sarcasm memes, therapy-speak, or corporate phrasing.

### 10.3 Diction Palette

Use frequently:
- oath, witness, fate, measure, debt, house, hearth, bronze, salt, grain, storm, omen
- captain, companion, herald, elder, supplicant, envoy, raider
- stand fast, yield, bear witness, pay the price, keep faith

Use sparingly (high-intensity scenes only):
- doom, curse, oracle, sacrilege, divine wrath

Avoid entirely:
- modern contractions-heavy banter and casual filler ("kinda," "sort of," "you know")
- fantasy faux-archaic clutter ("forsooth," "mayhaps")

### 10.4 Syntax & Rhythm

- **Default cadence (Xenophon-inspired):** Subject -> action -> consequence.
	- Example: "We cross at dawn, or we lose the ford by noon."
- **High drama cadence (Sophocles-inspired):** image -> judgment -> imperative.
	- Example: "The hills drink smoke already; choose now, and stand by it."
- **Balanced antithesis:** use paired contrasts for emphasis.
	- Example: "Better a scar with honor than soft hands in shame."
- **Rhetorical questions:** use for pressure, not decoration.
	- Example: "Will you guard your name, or sell it for one safe night?"

### 10.5 Character-Class Voice Guide

| Role | Style | Rule of Thumb | Example Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elder / Priest | Ritual, proverbial, image-heavy | Speak as if memory is older than the speaker | "No wall falls in one blow; it falls where vows were first neglected." |
| Captain / Officer | Crisp, tactical, duty-bound | Command through consequences | "Shields high on the left; the slope will break their charge." |
| Trader / Artisan | Exact, value-aware, worldly | Tie every choice to cost and scarcity | "Bronze I can weigh; promises I cannot store." |
| Scout / Ranger | Observational, spare, unsentimental | Report what is seen, then recommend action | "Three riders, no banner; they avoid the road and watch our fires." |
| Noble / Envoy | Formal, strategic, reputation-focused | Treat speech as negotiation | "I offer grain today and memory tomorrow; do not make me choose between them." |

### 10.6 Dialogue Structure Patterns

For pivotal scenes, use a 4-beat pattern:
1. **Claim**: what the speaker wants or believes
2. **Counterclaim**: the opposing duty/fact
3. **Cost**: what will be lost either way
4. **Choice**: a clear demanded action

For routine interactions (shops, travel, camp), use a 2-beat pattern:
1. **Situation**: concrete present condition
2. **Decision hook**: what the player can do now

### 10.7 Register by Screen Context

- **Battle barks:** 2-7 words, imperative, no ornament.
	- "Hold line."
	- "Spears forward."
	- "Right flank, brace."
- **World map travel lines:** observational + omen/weather + intent.
	- "Wind from the east; we make the pass before dusk."
- **Town dialogue:** warmer but still formal; include reciprocity, memory, obligation.
	- "Eat first, then speak; a hungry tongue breeds poor bargains."
- **Quest decisions:** explicit tradeoffs, no ambiguity about risk.
	- "Take the ford and lose men, or wait and lose the harvest."

### 10.8 Implementation Guardrails For Writers

- Keep player choices semantically distinct: honor, pragmatism, mercy, threat.
- Do not write "good/evil" caricatures; write conflicting obligations.
- Replace exposition dumps with contested dialogue where possible.
- One striking metaphor per scene is enough; do not stack poetic images.
- If a line can be said in plain words without losing force, prefer the plain line.

### 10.9 Quality Check (Pass/Fail)

A dialogue line passes if it is:
- **Clear in one read**
- **Rooted in material reality** (food, terrain, steel/bronze, weather, kinship, oath)
- **Value-loaded** (duty, cost, risk, reputation)
- **Tonally ancient without parody**

If a line sounds theatrical but says nothing actionable or meaningful, rewrite it.
